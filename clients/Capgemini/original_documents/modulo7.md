## Session Objective

**By the end of this module, the Engagement Manager will be able to:**

1. Understand the limitations of standard Copilot for internal document retrieval.
2. Use Copilot to effectively query and summarize official **MS Learn** documentation.
3. Translate complex technical concepts for non-technical stakeholders.

---

## 1. Information Retrieval Reality for EMs

**You have standard Copilot, not a custom Agent.**

* **The Limitation:** You cannot train the model on thousands of internal Capgemini PDFs permanently.
* **The Workaround:** You must bring the context *to* the chat, or direct Copilot to search the public web strategically (e.g., querying `learn.microsoft.com`) [@copilot2025].
* **The Value:** EMs often need to justify architectural decisions or compliance standards to clients. MS Learn is the ultimate source of truth for Microsoft ecosystem projects.

---

## Exercise 1: Policy Retrieval from MS Learn

**Context:** The client is nervous about data governance in their new Azure deployment. You need the latest official Microsoft stance to reassure them.
**Task:** Prompt Copilot to search MS Learn and summarize the governance best practices.

* **Draft Prompt to Try:**
  *"Search Microsoft Learn documentation for the latest best practices on 'Azure Data Governance'. Summarize the top 3 principles in a professional, reassuring email directed to a client's Chief Risk Officer. Include the source links at the bottom."*

---

## 2. Bridging the Technical Gap

**Engagement Managers are the bridge between developers and the business.**

* Developers speak in acronyms; clients speak in ROI and Risk.
* Copilot excels at analogy generation and technical summarization, making you a more effective communicator.

---

## Exercise 2: Technical Summarization via Analogy

**Context:** Your technical lead insists on refactoring a monolithic application into microservices, but the client doesn't understand why it costs \$50,000 and delays the project.
**Task:** Use Copilot to explain the concept using a relatable business analogy.

* **Draft Prompt to Try:**
  *"Explain the technical difference between a 'Monolithic Architecture' and 'Microservices'. Do not use heavy IT jargon. Instead, use an analogy related to managing a large corporate restaurant or kitchen. The goal is to justify to a non-technical CEO why migrating to microservices is worth the upfront investment."*
